# Independent Review Invitation — Project Brief

**The Boundaries of the Sayable / 言说的边界**  
**Version:** 2026-11-04 · Research only · No publication authorization

## Why we are asking

We are preparing an independent source-critical review of a small digital-history / computational social-science research project. The project studies how public institutional statements, historical publication records, administrative responses, and archived web visibility can be reconstructed without collapsing different kinds of evidence into one narrative.

We are **not** asking for endorsement of the project's political position or conclusions. We want a reviewer who is willing to identify unsupported claims, alternative explanations, source-dependence errors, and places where the evidence does not permit a conclusion.

## Review modules

A reviewer may accept only one module.

### Module A — Li Wenliang (2020) and Shanghai (2022)

- 38 source records across two evidence ledgers.
- 24 historical public-as-of views.
- 25 atomic claims.
- Core questions include source authenticity and publication time, distinctions between an eight-person public notice and an individual case, the sequence of separate institutional acts, the difference between reported policy predictions and later policy plans, and the distinction between a police investigation announcement and a judicial disposition.
- The reviewer is not asked to provide a legal opinion. Legal interpretation and translation parity are a separate review gate.

### Module B — Ai Fen / Renwu digital-archive history (2020)

- 20 source records and 30 archive observations.
- 29 atomic claims.
- Core questions include archived-page provenance, capture reproducibility, title and homepage states, derivative-copy dependence, and the difference between archive capture time and server-side edit or deletion time.
- The reviewer is not asked to infer censorship or intent from a missing page or link.

## Independence procedure

The review is staged:

1. **Phase I:** a conclusion-blind source reconstruction. The reviewer receives a locator index with neutral IDs and direct source/archive links, but not the author's claims, source-role labels, lineage groupings, or interpretations.
2. **Freeze:** the reviewer returns a dated first-pass worksheet.
3. **Phase II:** after the first pass is frozen, the author provides the claim register and historical time views for item-by-item adjudication.

This is not a fully blinded experiment: URLs and source content may reveal the subject. It is designed to prevent the author's conclusions from being supplied as the reviewer's starting point.

## What the reviewer can return

- A completed source/archival verification worksheet.
- A short chronology or source-dependence correction.
- Claim-level statuses: supported, partially supported, contradicted, inconclusive, or out of scope.
- A brief list of the most consequential weaknesses and recommended wording changes.

The reviewer may mark any item not checked or outside their expertise. Inaccessible material is not treated as disproven. Disagreement is preserved rather than averaged.

## Time and attribution

We can begin with a **bounded 60–90 minute scoping review** of the protocol and a few representative records. That scoping review does not count as G3 completion. A larger review would be agreed separately and may be limited to one module.

No public attribution, name, affiliation, quotation, or acknowledgement will be used without the reviewer's explicit permission. The review is not a coauthorship request and does not imply endorsement.
