## What question this tries to answer

Diagnostic statistics that flag misfitting items are usually computed on the
responses a benchmark actually observed. If some items are systematically less
observed for some respondents — in WILD the respondents are models — does the
*ranking* of items by misfit survive? And if we weight observed responses by the
inverse of their estimated probability of being observed (inverse-propensity
weighting, IPW), does the ranking come back?

Concretely: can IPW applied to item-level fit-score estimating ratios, holding
the Rasch predicted probabilities frozen, reduce the sensitivity to selective
observation that an earlier stage documented on fixed real response panels?

Three things are explicitly **not** the question, and the note does not claim
them:

1. whether WILD is naturally missing-not-at-random (MNAR);
2. whether genuinely abnormal items are detected;
3. whether this proposes a new IPW estimator.

## How it was set up

- **Data.** Two fixed response panels from the WILD benchmark
  (`kensho/WILD`), pinned by source SHA-256 `a05b8203…cd4aca`: IFEval/general,
  65 models × 528 items, and Winogrande/general, 65 models × 1,261 items. Rows
  are *models*, not people — WILD records model-by-item responses, and the models
  take the person role in the Rasch fit statistic. Each panel is a complete,
  non-constant subset (541 and 1,267 items before filtering), and the run record
  itself flags it as `not_representative_of_all_WILD_items`. The raw response
  file is not redistributed here.
- **Missingness.** Masks were generated from an already observed, dense panel at
  70% retained coverage, under MCAR, MAR and MNAR regimes at severities 0, 1.6
  and 2.8. Because the panels start out fully observed, these are *imposed*
  mechanisms: the recorded scope is "artificial selection with known pi", so the
  design measures counterfactual sensitivity, not which values are actually
  absent in the source data.
- **Instrument.** Rasch infit and outfit mean-square estimating ratios, with
  item difficulty from marginal maximum likelihood (MML). The estimand is the
  full-panel item-fit score conditional on frozen complete-data cross-fit
  predictions; a second arm re-estimates them on each masked panel instead.
- **Weighting arms.** `unweighted` (complete-case item mean-square ratio),
  `oracle_ipw` (inverse of the known artificial selection probability),
  `estimated_ipw` (a logistic response indicator fitted on synthetic index
  covariates only), and `oracle_ipw_clip10` (the oracle propensity floored at
  0.1).
- **Outcome measures.** Three readings of the same comparison, each against the
  complete-data run: the median absolute log shift of the fit ratios (oriented
  `unweighted − corrected`), the Spearman correlation of item severity ranks
  (`corrected − unweighted`), and the overlap of the top 10% flagged items
  (`corrected − unweighted`). Positive means the corrected ranking sits closer to
  the reference.
- **Replication.** 30 seeded mask draws per cell, seed basis 20261019, with a
  paired per-replication audit at the primary cells. A separate boundary series
  (10 draws, coverage 0.5 and 0.9, severities 0, 2.8 and 5.0) and a 3-draw refit
  arm are reported alongside.

## What the panels showed

Every number below is read from `stage7_primary_audit/stage7_paired_effects.csv`
and `stage7_refit_audit/stage7_paired_effects.csv` (30 paired replications per
cell, or 3 for the refit arm). The audit orients the shift as
`unweighted − corrected` and the other two as `corrected − unweighted`, so
positive means the corrected ranking sits closer to the complete-data reference.

| Cell | Median absolute log shift | Severity-rank Spearman | Top-10% overlap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Winogrande, MNAR 2.8, frozen, oracle IPW, infit | **+0.0977** (0.0951, 0.1002) · 30/30 improved | **+0.3484** (0.3390, 0.3577) · 30/30 | **+0.1480** (0.1368, 0.1593) · 30/30 |
| IFEval, same cell | +0.0386 (0.0351, 0.0420) · 30/30 | +0.2488 (0.2376, 0.2600) · 30/30 | +0.0503 (0.0383, 0.0624) · 28 improved, 2 tied |
| IFEval, MNAR 2.8, estimated IPW | −0.00020 (−0.00051, +0.00012) · 13 improved, 17 worsened | +0.00005 · 14 improved, 16 worsened | −0.0006 · 1 worsened, 29 tied |
| IFEval, MAR 2.8, oracle IPW | −0.0046 (−0.0062, −0.0030) · 5 improved, 25 worsened | **−0.0394** (−0.0492, −0.0295) · 2 improved, 28 worsened | +0.0195 · 19 improved, 4 worsened, 7 tied |
| Winogrande, MAR 2.8, oracle IPW | −0.0086 (−0.0096, −0.0076) · 0 improved, 30 worsened | **−0.0761** (−0.0816, −0.0706) · 0 improved, 30 worsened | −0.1273 · 0 improved, 30 worsened |
| Both panels, MCAR, oracle IPW | **0.0** (0, 0) · 30 ties | 0.0 · 30 ties | 0.0 · 30 ties |

Five things stand out:

- **Oracle weighting helps where the mechanism is the one it assumes.** On the
  Winogrande panel under MNAR, the median absolute log shift falls from 0.2090
  unweighted to 0.1113 weighted, and the severity ranking moves 0.348 closer to
  the complete-data ordering in every one of the 30 replications. IFEval moves in
  the same direction but about 40% as far, so the size of the gain is a property
  of the panel, not a universal constant.
- **Estimated propensities bought nothing.** Under the same MNAR regime the
  estimated-IPW arm sits at −0.00020 on the shift, with 13 replications improved
  and 17 worsened — indistinguishable from doing nothing. The clipped oracle arm
  is numerically identical to the unclipped one at this severity, so clipping
  never activated where it might have mattered. Knowing the mechanism is not the
  same as being able to estimate it from the observed panel.
- **Under MAR the same correction is a net harm, but not uniformly.** On
  Winogrande all three readings move away from the reference and not one of the
  30 replications improves. On IFEval the shift and the severity ranking worsen —
  25 and 28 of 30 replications respectively — while the top-10% overlap actually
  improves in 19 of 30. A weighting tuned to repair one mechanism biases the
  ranking when the missingness depends on something else, and which reading you
  look at can change the sign of the verdict.
- **The MCAR control is exactly zero, not approximately zero**: 30 ties in every
  reading on both panels. When the missingness does not depend on the item, there
  is nothing for the weights to undo, and the pipeline reports that rather than
  manufacturing a gain.
- **Refitting the probabilities instead of freezing them reverses the result.**
  On IFEval MNAR the refit arm moves the shift by −0.1543 (−0.1787, −0.1298),
  with 0 of 3 replications improved, and the severity ranking by −0.1853. Weight
  at the score layer does not repair a pipeline whose predicted probabilities were
  themselves re-estimated on the masked panel. This arm ran 3 replications and is
  reported as exploratory, not as a confirmatory result.

## Limits that matter more than the numbers

These are carried from the run records themselves, not added after the fact.

- **Reference, not truth.** "Diagnostic stability relative to the full-data
  baseline is not misfit-detection accuracy." The complete-data cross-fit is an
  empirical reference; no real misfit labels exist here and none were measured,
  so a smaller top-10% overlap is not evidence of worse anomaly detection.
- **Imposed mechanisms.** "No inference about whether natural WILD missingness is
  MNAR." The result describes how the diagnostic responds when such a mechanism
  is imposed on a dense panel.
- **The oracle is not deployable.** The MNAR oracle propensity uses target
  responses that a deployed correction could not observe, and "estimating logistic
  pi from response indicators alone cannot identify MNAR pi" — which is exactly
  what the flat estimated-IPW arm shows.
- **Synthetic covariates.** The MAR arm's covariates are synthetic index
  identifiers, not scientific predictor measurements, so its MAR regime is a
  structural convenience rather than a claim about observable predictors.
- **Finite-sample caution.** IPW fit scores are ratio estimators; unbiasedness is
  not guaranteed in finite samples, and no correction of the MML/JML
  parameter-estimation stage is claimed — the refit arm is the direct evidence
  for that limit.
- **Two panels, 65 models each.** Small, fixed, from one source, and selected as
  complete non-constant subsets. "Repeated masks on fixed matrices do not
  establish population generalization." The intervals cover mask draws
  conditional on each fixed panel only.
- **Exploratory comparisons.** Severities, panels and arms were compared without
  adjustment for multiple comparisons; the three-draw refit arm is exploratory on
  its replication count alone.
- **Null and negative results are the point.** No new estimator is claimed, and
  the MAR degradation and the reversed refit arm are reported with the same
  prominence as the MNAR gain.

## Rights and redistribution

The WILD dataset page labels the release Apache-2.0, while noting that original
source tasks may still carry task-level rights, and the permitted treatment on
record excludes redistribution of benchmark prompts. The AIMS measurement
database is gated behind acceptance of terms. Raw response files were therefore
kept out of every packaged stage; only shapes, hashes and aggregate statistics
appear here.
